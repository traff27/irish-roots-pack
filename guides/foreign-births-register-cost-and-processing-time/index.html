Foreign Births Register cost and processing time

Last checked 30 October 2026 against the official DFA page. Rules and fees can change: always check the DFA page before you post.

The full cost of registering from the UK, from the DFA fee to each certificate, and a realistic timeline from first order to registration.

In short

  • DFA fee: €278 if you're 18 or over, €153 if you're under 18. Paid online.
  • On top of that you pay for certificates: £12.50 each from the GRO in England and Wales, £12 from Scotland's People, £15 from GRONI and €20 from the HSE in Ireland (prices checked 30 October 2026).
  • The DFA currently takes about 12 months, processing applications in strict date order. Nobody outside the DFA can speed it up.

A worked example

Say you live in Leeds, your mother was born in Manchester and your grandmother was born in County Mayo. Your grandmother has died. On the grandparent route you'd typically need:

  • Your full birth certificate (GRO): £12.50
  • Your mother's full birth certificate (GRO): £12.50
  • Your mother's marriage certificate, if her surname changed (GRO): £12.50
  • Your grandmother's Irish birth certificate (HSE): €20 plus €5 postage
  • Your grandmother's marriage certificate, to link her maiden name (wherever she married)
  • Your grandmother's death certificate (wherever she died)

That's roughly six certificates before the DFA fee. If you already hold some originals, you may not need to order them again. This is an illustration, not a quote: what you need depends on your family.

Other small costs

  • 4 colour photographs (and 4 of yourself if you're applying for a child)
  • Recorded post to Ireland
  • Optional help, such as our £99 preparation service. It's not required: you can do the whole thing yourself.

How long it takes

StageTypical time
Ordering UK certificatesFrom a few days (GRO with an index number) to around 15 working days (GRO without an index number, Scotland's People standard)
Ordering Irish certificatesAround 5 working days to process a phone order, plus postage
Witnessing and postingDepends on you and your witness
DFA processingAbout 12 months, in strict date order

Both the DFA and Citizens Information currently give about 12 months. You may see older pages quoting 9 months: the current DFA page says 12. The DFA will contact you if it needs more documents, and its citizenship page says applications needing clarification or further documents take longer.

Can it be speeded up? No. The DFA processes applications in strict date order. The one exception it mentions is expectant parents whose baby may be born before the application is processed. They can contact the Customer Service Hub and should mark this clearly on the application.

When does citizenship start? On the date you're entered on the register, not your date of birth (Citizens Information). That matters if you're planning a family: children born before your registration date can't qualify through you.
